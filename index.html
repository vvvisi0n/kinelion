About Kinelion

The adaptive equipment industry needed a rethink.

For too long, adaptive tools have been designed around a clinical minimum. They function. They fulfill a requirement. But they fail the people who use them every day in ways that matter most: they look institutional, feel limiting, and are built by people who have never had to rely on them.

Kinelion was founded to correct that. Working directly with occupational therapy specialists and applying the principles of functional programming, we design adaptive solutions that meet the standard of the people who use them, not just the facilities that stock them.

Our B2B approach means we start where the need is most concentrated: rehabilitation hospitals, outpatient therapy clinics, and long-term care facilities. Every product we bring to market is tested, refined, and validated in real clinical environments before it reaches the people who need it.

Founded From Experience

Built by someone who lives it every day.

For over a decade, Victor Landing has lived with tetraplegia. In that time, he has watched technology transform nearly every corner of modern life. And yet one space has remained largely untouched. The adaptive equipment available to people with limited hand and upper body function has barely evolved. The products are clinical, uninspired, and built by people who have never had to rely on them.

Before tetraplegia, Victor built his career around human movement. As the founder of a private personal training practice, his work centered on functional programming: designing training systems that created real synergy between the body and its goals. His clients included executives, artists, and professionals who trusted him with their physical development in a space designed entirely around their privacy and goals. He delivered on what they asked for while solving what their body truly needed. That philosophy never left him.

When existing adaptive solutions failed to keep up with his active lifestyle, Victor did what he had always done. He engineered a better way. The first device he built was a wrist strap with a hook that allowed him to attach to cable systems at the gym. It broke under load. He rebuilt it stronger. That cycle of building, failing, and improving became the foundation of Kinelion.

Kinelion applies the same functional programming philosophy that defined his training practice, now combined with occupational therapy expertise, to build adaptive solutions that treat users as athletes and individuals, not patients to be accommodated.
